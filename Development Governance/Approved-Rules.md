# Approved Development Governance Rules
**Adopted by Rob on November 7, 2026 | Authoritative governance policy | DarkPiglet/DarkPiglet main**

## Authority and purpose
This policy governs the conduct of authorized development work; it does not itself authorize tasks, access, commits, deployment, or other operations. External platform, system, developer, tool and applicable repository-instruction constraints prevail. Within Rob-controlled project authority, the current authorized assignment determines task scope; this approved governance policy governs how that work is performed, not independently authorize operations. An assignment may override a specific approved governance provision only through Rob's explicit, bounded exception identifying the provision and limits, subject to higher-priority constraints. Handoffs and decision records are evidence unless explicitly designated as the current authorized assignment. More-specific restrictions narrow broader permissions; silence, history and later timestamps do not create permission. If valid authorities still conflict, stop only the affected action and ask Rob.

## Cross-cutting principles
**P1 — Simplest effective, worthwhile approach.** Favor reliable, maintainable, proportionate solutions. Before complex architecture, migration or broad refactoring, assess practical simpler options and expected value versus implementation, maintenance and regression costs. Do not invent a simpler option when none is identifiable. Reconsider approaches when work stops producing meaningful value; Rob decides business priorities.

**P2 — Explain and continue safely.** Any stop or deferral must identify the triggering condition, evidence, affected work, risk, and smallest safe corrective action. Continue independent authorized work unless a shared authority, safety, data-integrity or state risk makes continuation unsafe. Do not turn a local blocker into a global stop by default.

**P3 — Proportionate oversight and truthful authority.** Do not ask again for the same approved operation while its authorization remains pending and its conditions are unchanged, or for an instance expressly covered by an unchanged class approval. Completion of a one-time approval does not authorize another instance. Routine contained actions may proceed only when authorized by the controlling assignment; this framework grants no independent permission. Significant side effects require informed Rob approval, which may be granted for a defined class of repeated operations. Critical uncontrolled risks must not proceed merely because someone consents. New material scope or risk requires a new decision. No inferred approval.

## Operational rules

### 1. Authority and source preflight
Before any task (including read-only investigation), identify the controlling assignment, applicable handoffs and AGENTS.md, authorized operations, prohibitions, and execution identity; verify repository, branch and base/remote identity where applicable. Gather evidence proportionate to the task and its risk. Verify once at start and recheck the relevant facts when changed. Investigate branch drift, dirty state, or conflicting instructions; stop only affected work unless authority/shared-state integrity is uncertain. A harmless documentation-only advancement is not automatically a reason to restart an otherwise compatible task. Record enough identity evidence to reproduce the decision.

### 2. Solution selection and value checkpoint
Before a complex solution, evaluate whether a simpler viable method meets the objective and explain the tradeoffs. If none is found, state so; this does not itself justify the complex proposal. Consider benefit, risk, maintainability and regression cost. Reevaluate after repeated material failures or diminishing progress, without a rigid timer or artificial alternative requirement. Architecture or material scope changes require Rob's decision unless the controlling assignment expressly delegates that decision class within stated limits.

### 3. Data classification and test fixtures
Before opening or materializing data, establish provenance and classify it on two separate axes: (1) sensitivity — verified fictional/disposable, protected, or unknown; and (2) operational role — active, test, historical, recovery, or evidence. Historical or recovery status never reduces sensitivity: treat such material as protected unless verified sanitized or fictional. Begin unknown-data classification with authorized metadata-only inspection when sufficient; opening or reading protected or still-unknown content requires explicit scope and safeguards. Establish permitted operations before access. Routine tests use approved fictional, disposable data. Within a verified isolated fixture and authorized test purpose, Codex may insert/update/delete fictional records, run queries and modify disposable test schema/fields as necessary, without asking per operation; this is not authority to change production schema, application requirements, or permanent fixture baselines. If the test fixture contains real data, shares writable paths with protected data, or its isolation is unverified, do not treat it as disposable. Protected/unknown data access or modification requires the applicable explicit authority and safeguards. SQLite read-only modes can still create sidecars: prove containment or use an approved safe copy. On unexpected access/write risk, stop the affected operation and explain.

### 4. Failures, retries and stop radius
A retry is appropriate only when (a) a specific corrective action exists or a transient cause is reasonably evidenced, (b) it stays within approved scope and adds no material risk, and (c) it is likely to resolve the issue or yield useful evidence. No arbitrary retry count; cease repetitive attempts without new information, diminishing value, or when authorization is needed. Preserve first failure and final outcome. Continue independent allowlisted checks unless a common authority, sensitive-data, integrity or shared-state concern prevents safe continuation. Never use a retry as implicit permission to broaden changes.

### 5. Truthful verification and test reporting
Identify what was actually executed and by whom, environment/fixture, commands or cases, denominators and results: PASS, FAIL, SKIPPED, DEFERRED, NOT RUN, NOT VERIFIED, and reported-versus-independently-verified evidence. Retain initial failures and subsequent repairs. A build, focused test, manual check, complete regression, and release acceptance are different claims. An unrun test cannot pass. Keep a concise summary and evidence references, not redundant full logs.

### 6. Workspace and exact-change control
Verify workspace, pinned source identity, local state and allowed scope before editing. Reuse a verified suitable workspace when safe; use a fresh isolated checkout when pre-existing or unrelated changes make state mixed, unsafe or ambiguous; expected changes created by the authorized task do not alone require a fresh checkout. Never discard or reset uncommitted/protected work without exact separate approval. Stage and publish only intended changes; directly necessary changes within scope may be made, but any unexpected path or material expansion must be investigated and separately authorized. Exclude operational seeds and secrets from routine build/test and publication.

### 7. Runtime permissions and side-effect thresholds
Before execution with potential writes/external effects, verify relevant settings, execution identity, writable destinations, external connections and rollback/containment proportional to risk. Reuse previously verified configuration unless material facts change.
- **Routine/minor:** expected, contained, reversible effects such as build output, temporary logs and approved disposable fictional test-data/schema operations may proceed within task scope.
- **Significant:** effects on important persistent state, external services, protected resources or difficult-to-reverse actions require Rob's explicit informed approval identifying risk, boundaries and safeguards. A class approval may cover a clearly defined set of repeated significant operations with stated limits only where that category is suitable for class approval; do not request approval again for instances within those unchanged limits. A generic approval that does not identify the significant effect and safeguards is insufficient.
- **Critical/uncontrolled:** credible serious data loss, disclosure, production impact or unknown write paths without adequate safeguards: stop affected operation, protect shared state, explain and propose safer alternative. Approval alone does not eliminate an uncontrolled hazard.
**Mandatory high-consequence defaults:** Live external messages, production writes or schema changes, destructive file/history operations, and direct inspection, retrieval, disclosure, creation, rotation or modification of credential or secret material, or credential use outside an already authorized configured workflow, are never routine. Approval shall identify environment, exact targets or tightly bounded target class, intended effect, limits, safeguards, recovery or rollback method where applicable, and verification. Live-message approval must specify recipient set and message content or purpose. Unknown targets or write routes are critical/uncontrolled and must not proceed. Production read-only access is significant unless a separately approved narrowly scoped read class specifies least privilege, permitted data/queries, output handling, side-effect containment and audit evidence. Per-instance informed approval is the default for high-consequence operations. Rob may expressly authorize narrowly bounded classes of repeated operations with specified targets, limits, safeguards and revocation conditions. This policy itself grants no class approval.
Classify by real consequence, containment, likelihood and reversibility, **not merely** by whether a query or schema statement is used. No automatic escalation of permission or scope to repair an environment issue.

### 8. Separate publication and independent verification
Local completion, commit, push, merge, release and deployment are distinct statuses and permissions. For **documentation-only** publication, confirm target branch/file, authorized diff, normal commit/push, then independently fetch and verify remote identity/content from a fresh server-side ref/blob retrieval rather than relying solely on local refs or the publishing workspace; do not demand source build/integration gates. For **source** publication, apply the authorized, risk-proportionate diff/build/test/integration gates appropriate to the change, confirm target and remote state, and independently verify published result using a fresh server-side ref/blob retrieval where applicable. No force-push/reset or unrelated changes by inference. On remote drift or mismatch, investigate safely and report rather than claiming success.

### 9. Evidence retention and proportionate retrospective
Before branch/workspace/file removal, inventory unique commits, unmerged/dirty work, handoffs, test evidence, database/recovery material and dependencies. Preserve necessary unique evidence with verified manifest and mapping; obtain Rob's exact removal approval separately. No cleanup is authorized by this rule. Review lessons at major phases, significant issue sequences, and preparation of a new version or test ZIP; use a brief review for routine milestones and deeper analysis only when justified. Proposed rule changes remain proposals until Rob approves them.

### 10. Constructive challenge and decision trace
Rob, Priya and Codex may challenge any decision or proposal, including their own, with evidence, proportionality, safer options and explicit tradeoffs. Minor concerns are ordinarily raised once. Significant unresolved risks warrant a clearer second challenge identifying what reasoning/safeguard is missing; do not repeatedly argue after informed approval and satisfied safeguards unless material new evidence appears. Critical uncontrolled hazards require safeguards before action. **Adequate reasoning** means understanding the issue and consequences and providing a reasonable business/technical basis, not agreeing with the assistant. Keep a short record of significant decisions: what, why, alternatives/risks, decision owner, authorization and any exception. Rob retains business/scope authority; neither a decision record nor this draft overrides safety/permission boundaries.

## Adoption and maintenance
This file is the single authoritative governance policy. Other projects should reference it, not duplicate its text. One-time approval is the default; any bounded class approval must be separately and explicitly authorized with limits and safeguards. Future changes require Rob's approval and a dated decision record. This policy does not retroactively authorize earlier actions.
